# 10 — Enemies and Bestiary

> Part of the Taimanin research reference. See [README](README.md) for sources and
> continuity tags. This document looks at the setting's beings *as opponents*.
> What they are is in [06 — Races and Species](06-races-and-species.md); who
> commands them is in [05 — Factions](05-factions.md).

## 1. How opposition works in this franchise

Five patterns recur across every entry, and they matter more for design than any
single monster:

1. **The enemy wants captives, not corpses.** Antagonists overwhelmingly aim to
   take Taimanin alive — to break, convert, sell, clone or exhibit them. Defeat in
   the source material is a *state change* (capture, corruption, forced service),
   not a game over, and the "bad ending" branches are a pillar of the adult VNs.
2. **Fights are rarely fair.** The heroine is usually stronger than anything in
   the room. She loses to hostages, betrayal by her own side, poison and
   aphrodisiac gas, pre-set traps, sealed powers, or an informant in the village.
3. **Corruption is cumulative.** Drugs, modification and implants carry over from
   one encounter to the next; a past capture leaves permanent weak points.
4. **Human enemies are as dangerous as demons.** Corrupt officials, corporate
   security, rival ninja and foreign special forces drive most plots.
5. **Three enemy "tech trees"** — mechanical (UFS), biological (Chinese Union) and
   demonic/magical (Nomad and the Demon Realm) — are mixed inside most enemy
   forces.

## 2. Enemy tiers

Calibrated against the fan wiki's unofficial 0–7 power scale
([07](07-powers-and-ninja-arts.md) §8).

| Enemy tier | Scale | Role in an encounter | Typical members |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fodder** | 1 | Arrive in groups; dangerous only by number or surprise | Gang thugs, security guards, ordinary soldiers, low orcs, goblins, imps, zombies, basic skeletons, small drones |
| **Regular** | 2 | The standard fight | Trained soldiers in exoskeletons, orc warriors, beastman enforcers, ghouls, boosted soldiers, clones, rogue rank-and-file ninja, animal drones |
| **Veteran** | 3 | Squad leaders; one needs attention | Elite soldiers, high orcs, ogres, oni mercenaries, cyborg soldiers, arena fighters, onmyoji or exorcist renegades |
| **Elite** | 4 | Mini-boss; a named threat | Cyborg agents, Raiden-class suits, High Ghouls, chimeras, walking tank drones, death knights, veteran rogue Taimanin |
| **Champion** | 5 | Boss of a mission | Top rogue ninja, Hell Knights, rakshasa and valkyries, vampire and wraith officers, senior demonologists with prepared ground |
| **Legend** | 6 | Arc boss; "could face a small army" | The strongest Hell Knights, traitor masters, awakened or demonized Taimanin, lesser nobles |
| **Sovereign** | 7 | Story-final boss; "could bring down small nations" | Edwin Black, the great lords of the Demon Realm, incubus kings, ancient dragons |
| **Beyond** | X | Not fought directly; survived, sealed or banished | Gods and devils, Vessels of the Void, Black at full power, Brain Flayer elders with their relics |

## 3. Human opponents

| Type | Affiliation | How they fight | Weak point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Street gangs and mafia soldiers** | Tokyo Kingdom gangs, Dragon Gate, Nine Dragons | Guns, numbers, home ground; hell-tech drugs | Morale; no answer to ninja arts |
| **Corporate and private security** | Nomad fronts, Washizu Group | Modern arms, surveillance, gas traps | Predictable |
| **Slavers and "trainers"** | Under Eden, arenas, House of Greed | Restraints, drugs, contracts; avoid a straight fight | Weak once the captive is free |
| **Corrupt officials and politicians** | Japanese government, both parties | Never fight; they revoke backing, leak plans, hold hostages | Exposure |
| **Regular soldiers** | JGSDF, UFS forces | Plasma rifles, vehicles, discipline | Ordinary humans |
| **Exoskeleton infantry** | UFS (Bone → Alexander → Claudius); Japan's **Kanda Brigade** (Raiden, Shusui) | Armour and firepower on a tank's level; the Kanda Brigade occupied Gosha itself `[VN]` | Mobility, joints, the pilot |
| **Cyborg soldiers (CS)** | UFS Task Force G | Bodies rebuilt for war; particle detectors; drone support | Instability from over-modification |
| **Cyborg agents (CA)** | UFS DSO and others | Covert operatives built to match Taimanin one-on-one | Few in number |
| **Boosted soldiers** | UFS, Chinese Union | Drugged, helmet-controlled humans or orcs; no fear | Crude; no initiative |
| **Clones** | Chinese Union, Dragon Gate | Copies of strong fighters, sometimes of the heroines themselves; cybernetic or magical add-ons | No Ninja Art; unstable sense of self |
| **Demonologists** ("dark doctors") | Nomad and freelancers | Prepared laboratories, bioweapons, grafts, implanted failsafes in their victims | Fragile in person |
| **Witches and magicians** | Amidahara's guild, freelancers | Powerful, varied magic | Slow casting |
| **Rogue Taimanin** (*nukenin*) | Individuals and bands | Everything a Taimanin can do, plus inside knowledge | Same as any Taimanin |
| **Rival ninja forces** | The **Nisha Ninja Troops** ("Righteous Fuuma"); Toyo Momochi's rogue ninja (nanomachine-boosted foot soldiers, the CD virus) `[RPGX]` `[AT]`; the old **Fuuma** under Danjo; the Igawa revolt | Ninja arts turned on Gosha | Feuds and succession disputes |
| **Renegade onmyoji and exorcists** | — | Barriers, seals, shikigami | Need catalysts and set-up time |

## 4. Machines

| Unit | Maker | Class | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Remote and spider drones** | UFS; terrorist copies | Fodder–Regular | Cheap ground drones; a common type stuns then executes with high voltage. |
| **Turret W series** | UFS | Regular | Walker, crawler (long-range bombardment) and floater variants; shoot anything not tagged friendly. |
| **Animal drones** | UFS and others | Regular | Tank-grown cyborg wasps, dogs and ants with guns. Illegal. |
| **FSD Gargoyle** | Task Force G | Support | Silent flying scout feeding a real-time "Eagle Eye" battle picture to cyborg troops. |
| **FSD Cancer** | Task Force G | Veteran | Agile unmanned walker with a tactical AI. |
| **FSD Dauntless** | Task Force G | Elite | Slower walker with a small tank's armour and guns. |
| **FSD Crius / AQ-3 Iron Ghost** | Task Force G | Elite–Champion | Large multi-legged AI tanks; the Iron Ghost's AI uses demon-realm tech and can "trouble a small nation". |
| **AQ-9 Killer Bee** | UFS | Champion (boss) | Flying drone that predicts attacks and dodges; guns and rockets `[RPGX]`. |
| **Androids / synthetics** | Humans, Brain Flayers | Varies | Free-willed prototypes exist. |
| **Golems and automatons** | Witches, magicians | Regular–Elite | Guard constructs; follow orders literally. |

**Universal machine weakness:** electromagnetic arts (Lightning, Elec, Magnet),
hacking, and miasma, which makes AI disobey.

## 9. Conflicts and uncertainty

- Class assignments here are this document's reading of narrative descriptions and
  the wiki's unofficial tier list; no game publishes a unified ranking.
- Enemy rosters of the online games (`[AT]` stage enemies, `[RPGX]` event bosses)
  are only partly recorded on the lore wiki; the gameplay wikis that list them
  were not reachable (see README).
- Several enemies appear in one continuity only.

## 10. Design notes — structures worth borrowing

1. **Defeat as a branching state, not a reload.** Capture, rescue, escape and
   lasting consequences give a "fail forward" loop that suits a living-world sim.
2. **Asymmetric threat.** Weak enemies stay relevant by changing the *conditions*
   of the fight — hostages, gas, sealed skills, ambush terrain — rather than by
   having bigger numbers.
3. **Three enemy families with crossed weaknesses**: machines (weak to
   electromagnetic powers, hacking and miasma), bioweapons (weak to their
   controllers and to instability), demons (weak to the heroes' own energy and to
   species-specific conditions).
4. **A unit that counters stealth** (particle-tracking hounds, detectors, scout
   drones) so that infiltration has a real counter-play.
5. **Honourable elites versus dishonourable masterminds.** Champions who duel
   fairly and schemers who never fight at all are both boss types, and they ask
   for different player skills.
6. **Leaders whose death collapses a force** (the wraith hive) alongside leaders
   who cannot be killed at all (Otherworld beings) — two clear objective types.
7. **Recurring nemeses** justified in-fiction by body-swapping, cloning,
   resurrection and banishment.
