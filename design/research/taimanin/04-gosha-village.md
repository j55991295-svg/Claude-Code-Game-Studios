# 04 — Gosha Village and Gosha Academy

> Part of the Taimanin research reference. See [README](README.md) for sources and
> continuity tags. This is the franchise's home base and the place the original
> request named as a starting point, so it is covered in more detail than other
> locations.

## 1. Gosha Village (五車町, *Gosha-machi*)

### 1.1 Summary

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| What it is | The hidden headquarters town of the Taimanin, formed by gathering ninja clans that used to be scattered across Japan |
| Where | Somewhere concealed in the **Kanto region**, among old-growth forest and mountains |
| Population | About **10,000** (Taimanin and their families) |
| Controlled by | The Gosha Taimanin, under Section Three's oversight; funded secretly by the government |
| Leader | The Taimanin Chief Commander, who is also academy headmaster and so the **de facto mayor** — Asagi Igawa in most continuities |
| First shown | *Taimanin Murasaki* |
| Nearest town | Maesaki City — about three hours away with several bus and train changes |

**The name.** *Gosha* means "five wheels": **five** for the five phases of yin-yang
thought (wood, fire, earth, metal, water) that underlie ninja arts, and **wheel** for
the throwing wheel — the shuriken.

### 1.2 Concealment and defences

- Hidden by a combination of **modern stealth technology and ninja arts**.
- Ringed by the **Virgin Forest** (about 30 km² of protected ancient woodland) and
  the **Gosha mountains**, which are hard to climb even for a trained Taimanin.
- Infiltration is considered close to impossible; Sakura remarks that even her
  Shadow Art would struggle to get in unseen.
- Government suppression keeps its existence out of public knowledge.
- Legal cover: Taimanin on duty are exempted from liability for deaths and damage
  under an emergency clause of a national secrets act.

### 1.3 History

- **Before Gosha.** Clans lived in their own villages (Igawa Village, Fuuma Village,
  Koukawa Village) and fought each other as often as demons.
- **Unification (to c. 2065).** After a political crisis and rising Demon Realm
  interference, the government financed a single unified village on a site of old
  significance to the clans. It grew from several smaller villages merging.
- **Leadership struggle.** The Fuuma clan first acted as go-between with the
  government; control passed to the Igawa clan and its elders; the **Fuuma Revolt**
  (2068) and, in some continuities, the **Igawa Revolt** (2070) followed. The elders'
  power was eventually broken and authority settled on the Igawa clan head as
  Chief Commander.
- **"Gosha Taimanin" vs everyone else.** Members call any unaffiliated ninja a
  **rogue Taimanin** — a label of affiliation, not necessarily of morality. Critics
  call Gosha Taimanin "dogs of the government".
- **Legal status.** Funded illegally at first; made official in 2075 when the
  Taimanin were integrated into Section Three as secret state agents.
- **2082 `[VN]`.** Occupied by the Kanda Brigade on the prime minister's orders;
  about half the student body killed; liberated after his exposure.

### 1.4 What lies beneath

- In the north once stood **Narashino Castle**, seat of the medieval tyrant
  Nagatake Narashino, who controlled a Demon Gate of his own. Only a stone marker
  remains.
- Under the village are **older ruins** once venerated as a temple of the dawn
  goddess **Ushas**. In truth they were built by a pacifist splinter group of
  **Brain Flayers** about 10,000 years ago. Their queen, **Marjaana**, later became
  the Demon Realm's "Wise Lord"; a guardian, **Eos**, still attacks intruders in
  the deep levels.
- The ruins contain an **inactive Demon Gate** — a few broken stones that nobody,
  Taimanin or demon, has managed to restart. In `[SQ]` it is briefly reactivated
  and mind-controlled demons pour into the village until it is destroyed.
- Access points are in the oldest parts of the village and anywhere that goes deep
  underground, notably the academy **library**.
- `[GOGO]` adds a **Labyrinth Dungeon** that appears under the village.

## 2. Gosha Academy (五車学園, *Gosha Gakuen*)

### 2.1 Summary

Both the **command centre** of the Gosha Taimanin and their **training school**.
Missions are issued from the headmaster's office. A state secret, like the village.
*Taimanin Saga* notes that the academy concept was modelled on the X-Men's school.

**Two campuses**

| | Old academy | New academy |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Edge of the village, by the Virgin Forest | Nearer the village centre |
| Look | Wooden buildings | White façade |
| Features | Very large underground HQ; purpose-built mock urban blocks for combat training | Built over the ancient temple complex; large library; **Holographic Training Room** replaces the mock town |
| Status | In use in the old visual novels; abandoned and reused as the girls' dormitory in `[RPGX][AT]` | Standard in `[ZERO][RPGX][AT]` |

**Facilities**: classrooms, principal's office, separate dormitories, baths, pool,
large yard, cafeteria, gym, medical and research wing, underground headquarters,
library, equipment workshops.

### 2.2 Structure of study

- A through-school: primary, lower and upper secondary and higher education. The
  oldest students are university age and typically graduate into squad captaincies
  or teaching.
- **Juniors** — the youngest cohort; rarely seen. The focus at this stage is learning
  to control **Taima particles**; unique arts seldom awaken this early. Missions are
  rare and simple.
- **Seniors** — from about 16–17 upward to 19–20 and beyond. May take missions
  without an older Taimanin, lead squads, and instruct juniors.
- **The awakening rule.** A senior who never awakens a ninja art cannot become a full
  Taimanin and must leave the academy — at worst the village. (Kyousuke Sawaki,
  Asagi's fiancé, was expelled this way. Rare exceptions: Toyo Momochi, who never
  awakened, and Kuro Yatsu, who awakened late and returned after army service.)
- Semesters start at the end of October `[RPGX]`.
- Guest and exchange students from abroad are sometimes admitted.
- Culture: students and teachers are *encouraged* to spar to knockout anywhere on
  the grounds, at any time, to keep everyone sharp.

> **Source conflict — ages.** The old visual novels show students in their late teens
> to early twenties (Murasaki was a top student at about 20–21). `[AT]` includes
> named characters aged 14–15 in non-adult content and says the course takes about
> three years. `[GOGO]` jokes that every student is over 20. There is no single
> consistent rule.

**Uniform and grades**

- Old continuity: one uniform for all — green jacket, white tie, black skirt or
  trousers.
- `[ZERO][RPGX][AT]`: white and blue, with **tie colour marking the year group**.
  Yellow = preparatory. Blue, green and red rotate: a cohort keeps its colour
  until graduation and the next intake inherits the freed colour.
- Taimanin suits are individual and tailored to the wearer's art, but are for
  practical classes only.

### 2.3 Curriculum

| Course | Content |
|---|---|
| General education | Mathematics, English, Japanese literature, history, politics, PE |
| **Botany** | Plants of both realms; poisons and antidotes |
| **Combat** | *Taijutsu* (unarmed), *shurikenjutsu*, *kenjutsu*; mock battles against instructors whether or not one's art has awakened; pain resistance; optional weapon-style classes |
| **Espionage** | Disguise, infiltration, intelligence gathering, stratagems, swimming and underwater work, escape and concealment, tactics |
| **Seduction arts** | Using seduction as a weapon and enduring it if captured; knowing one's own weaknesses. Low-risk virtual reality is used for training |
| **Equipment and research** | Tools and gear; some students build their own |
| **Camping training** | Survival in the forest |
| **Field training** | Supervised real work — typically low-priority VIP protection |
| **Special course** | Optional five-day annual camp for would-be elites: theory in the morning, field exercises in the afternoon. Most fail and retake it over several years |

Training aids: the **Holographic Training Room**, **Taima automatons** (training
dummies), and a staff masseur expert in Taimanin-specific muscle care, acupuncture
and moxibustion.

**School rule of note:** no adult material on campus except for research and
art-training purposes.

in [08 — Characters](08-characters.md).

### 2.5 The Seven Mysteries `[RPGX]`

A school-ghost-story arc (written by the creator of *Corpse Party*). After the art
student **Yuka Aozora** fell to her death by the great mirror on the third-floor
landing, seven phenomena began to occur when the school is empty at night. A stamp
book fills in as each is witnessed; at the sixth it teleports the reader away.

1. A painting in the art room that draws the viewer into a trance.
2. A woman on the audiovisual-room screen who drives viewers mad — the footage
   appears to come from *other timelines*.
3. A pale hand from an out-of-order stall in the girls' toilets.
4. A spirit by the toilets demanding "red paper or white paper?", each with a curse.
5. Training dummies in the store-room that move, multiply and "kill" in a dream.
6. **The great mirror**, a door to the other world, whose smiling reflections climb
   out and try to drag the viewer in.
7. Unknown — said to be whatever the witness fears most.

Resolved by repairing Yuka's damaged self-portrait. The wiki speculates the mirror
world is a pocket dimension outside normal space-time that links to bad-ending
versions of the same characters.

## 3. Organisation housed here

| Level | Detail |
|---|---|
| Oversight | **Section Three** of the public-safety ministry (director Nobushige Yamamoto) sets state missions and funds and equips the village |
| Command | **Taimanin Chief Commander** = headmaster |
| Units | **Taimanin squads** (班) of mostly two or three, which replaced the old clan-only teams. Specialist formations exist, e.g. the ten-group **Fire Squad Unit** of fire-art users |
| Special units | **Independent Commando Unit** `[RPGX]`, **Taimanin Task Force** `[AT]`, **Dungeon Squad** `[GOGO]`, the player's **Squad** `[SQ]` |
| Clans | Still the social fabric: Igawa (ruling), Yatsu (serves Igawa), Fuuma and its branches `[RPGX][AT]`, Mizuki, Akiyama, Kamimura, Koukawa survivors, and dozens of smaller families |
| Tensions | Old clan grudges; resentment of the Fuuma; conservative elders; the Internal Affairs Bureau; periodic budget fights with politicians |

## 4. Design notes — why this works as a starting hub

1. **Small, legible, layered.** A 10,000-person hidden town with one institution
   at its heart, a handful of named shops, a ring of wilderness and a buried secret.
   Everything a first hub needs and nothing it does not.
2. **Hub services map cleanly to places.** Headmaster's office = quest board;
   training room = tutorial and repeatable combat; clinic = healing and upgrades;
   workshop = gear; sweet shop and pub = social scenes; library = lore and the
   door to the dungeon below; dojo = weapon-style trainer; bathhouse = recovery.
3. **Progression gates built into the fiction.** Tie colour by year, the
   particle-control stage before any art, the pass/fail awakening rule, the
   optional elite camp — a ready-made rank ladder.
4. **Wilderness difficulty bands.** Valley (safe) → forest (training) → mountains
   (hard) → northern castle ruin (dangerous) → ruins under the library (endgame).
5. **Time-of-day content.** The Seven Mysteries only happen at night in an empty
   school.
6. **A dormant gate under the starting town** is a natural long-term hook for a
   game about Gates.
7. **Social structure as system.** Clans with heads, stewards and elders; squads
   of two or three; a commander balancing government, clans and students.
