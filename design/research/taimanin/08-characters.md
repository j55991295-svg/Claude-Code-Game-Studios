# 08 — Characters

> Part of the Taimanin research reference. See [README](README.md) for sources and
> continuity tags. **Asagi Igawa has her own document:**
> [09 — Asagi Igawa](09-asagi-igawa.md). Arts and techniques named here are
> explained in [07 — Powers, Ninja Arts and Magic](07-powers-and-ninja-arts.md).

## 1. Reading guide

- The fan wiki lists about 1,300 characters, most of them gacha units from the
  online games. This document covers the roughly fifty who carry the plots.
- Names are given in Western order (given name first).
- **Characters differ by continuity** — ages, allegiances and even whether they are
  alive. Where it matters the entry says which continuity it describes. Birth
  years are the wiki's, and commonly shift by four or five years between the VN
  and online timelines.
- Entries are factual and non-graphic. The adult VNs put most heroines through
  capture-and-corruption routes and multiple "bad endings"; those are noted only
  where they shape the character's later story.

## 10. Conflicts and uncertainty

- **Ages and birth years** differ between the VN and online timelines for most of
  the younger cast (Yukikaze 2060 vs 2064–65; Rinko 2059 vs 2063).
- **Alive or dead** depends on continuity for Black, Oboro, Fürst, Danjo, Ingrid
  and others; VN endings also branch.
- **Kotaro Fuuma** is effectively two characters (§6).
- **Shiranui** is villain, victim, ruler or headmistress depending on the entry.
- Whether Koukawa Oboro's survival was planned before *Asagi 3* is a retcon
  question ([02 — Timeline](02-timeline.md)).

## 11. Design notes — structures worth borrowing

1. **Every major character = one art + one weapon + one flaw.** The flaw is usually
   the story hook: uncontrolled power, pride, guilt, mixed blood, a missing parent.
2. **Mentor and successor pairs** across three generations give a ready-made
   recruitment and legacy structure.
3. **Characters defined by what they lack** — no art, no sight, no limbs, no soul —
   are among the most popular in the cast, and each lack is compensated by a
   distinct play style.
4. **The same person reused across continuities in a different role** is a direct
   precedent for a multiverse game: alternate-world versions of a known character
   are an accepted idea in this fiction.
5. **Enemies who become staff** (the demonologist, the Hell Knight ally) show a
   path from boss fight to service NPC.
6. **Family trees do the plotting**: half-siblings on opposite sides, adopted
   heirs, disinherited heads of house.
